# Work log

- Established the initial Earth Through Time shell: a compact timeline header and a CSS-only deep-space background. Kept the stage empty so later scroll and globe work has a clean foundation.
- Added the opening hero with a centred title and a single-sentence introduction that explains both the story's span and the scroll mechanic without competing with the future globe.
- Built the core scroll-as-time experience around one sticky, rotating 3D Earth. Twelve weighted eras now crossfade through procedural surface textures while a shared typed timeline updates the date and short explanation; the present is intentionally compressed near the end, and the pure mapping is unit-tested.
- Kept a CSS globe and chronological era list as the no-WebGL/no-JavaScript fallback, so the same story remains readable rather than collapsing to an empty stage.
- Expanded the narrative from 12 to 42 events after the initial version felt too sparse beside a full formation-to-destruction timeline. Added geological boundaries, all five major mass-extinction intervals, evolutionary transitions, human-scale events and more future states while retaining the non-linear scale.
- Switched procedural globe textures to a six-item on-demand cache. The denser event list now adds narrative depth without allocating a separate 1024-pixel planet texture for every stop at startup.
- Rebalanced the chronology after the past dominated 93% of the scroll. “Now” sits at 58%, leaving 42% for 27 researched future events (62 total): conditional glaciation, tectonic scenarios, a brighter Sun, eclipse loss, atmospheric deoxygenation, revised vegetation limits, ocean and microbial decline, and distinct late-Sun phases through the white-dwarf era. Model-dependent claims are labelled as such, including the newly uncertain fate of Earth during the Sun’s giant phases.
- Hardened the agent harness so `spec/invariants.test.ts` remains immutable and additional tests require an explicit user request; removed the obsolete starter example as directed by `spec/README.md`.
- Trimmed `CLAUDE.md` to the active work, dependency-safety, and process-logging guidance, removing the opening course overview and everything after the process-logging section.
- Replaced the visibly tiled CSS star pattern with a deterministic canvas field whose positions, sizes, brightness and colour temperatures vary naturally without repeating. Removed the era-coloured background bloom and every dedicated globe halo/rim layer so the planet now has a clean silhouette.
- Increased the starfield to roughly four times its prior population, separating it into a faint deep field and brighter foreground. Expanded the temperature palette, size hierarchy, rare diffraction details and occasional close pairs to add variety without restoring an artificial pattern.
- Rebuilt the planet as a layered renderer: higher-resolution textured terrain, an independent ocean/specular mask, a separately rotating cloud shell, and a true atmospheric-scattering shell now respond to the same era transitions without flattening into one painted sphere.
- Reworked the lighting and colour pipeline with ACES tone mapping, stronger daylight, restrained ocean glints, terrain relief, biome variation and a brighter atmospheric limb. The no-WebGL globe received matching depth, cloud and atmosphere treatments so its fallback no longer reads as a flat disc.
- Added local day, ocean, cloud and night-light maps from the official Three.js example texture collection for the present-day Earth, while preserving generated era-specific geography for deep history and the far future.
- Reworked the late-stage Sun from a translucent blurred wash into an opaque stellar disc with a white-gold photosphere, darker limb, subtle surface variation and a broader coloured corona. Its existing timeline strength and size transitions remain intact.
- Split the final white dwarf from the red-giant treatment. The old warm, spotted miniature disc now crossfades into a compact blue-white photosphere with a sharp limb, cool halo and fine diffraction rays as the giant sheds its envelope.
- Made the present moment physically linger for a little over one viewport of native scrolling, with a filling hold indicator so the resistance feels intentional rather than broken. Added a present-only reflection that compares written history to the final tenth of a 24-hour Earth and frames humanity as both vanishingly brief and capable of choosing what follows; the mobile present composition gives the reflection extra stage space so it stays readable at the marked viewport.
- Added a cratered Moon that forms during the Moon-forming impact, remains beside Earth through the timeline, and shares Earth’s interpolated late-life heating so it turns red and develops a matching glow as the Sun expands.
- Corrected the white-dwarf ending so the Moon fades out through Earth’s uncertain-fate transition and is absent once the Sun is dead, matching the deliberately unresolved final state.
- Reserved 1.1 viewport-heights of pinned scrolling after the white-dwarf state reaches completion, giving the final copy time to be read before the timeline releases into the conclusion.
- Retargeted the conclusion's return link from the vertically centred title to the top-level page landmark, so activating it returns to the document's true beginning.
- Added a dependency-free performance regression suite that budgets the built payload, compressed client bundle, request graph and asset sizes, and benchmarks the scroll-to-era mapping hot path. Added `pnpm test:performance` and documented that the checks measure `dist/`.
- Added correctness coverage for timeline data and interpolation, the built chronological fallback, and interactive scroll-driven DOM/accessibility updates. Made `pnpm test` build before testing so built-page checks are reliable, and documented focused and full test commands in `CLAUDE.md`.
- Added the new correctness suite to `PROCESS.md` as a harness-level feedback improvement, including why building before built-output tests matters and the passing 35-test result.
- Reverted the scroll-resistance work from merged PRs #9 and #10 and closed unmerged PR #11 after real use exposed halted scrolling, jumping copy, and jarring transitions; restored the simpler pre-PR-9 timeline and documented why interaction feedback outweighed green logic tests.
- Fixed a visual-state inheritance bug found during direct use: the +10, +50, +500 and +600 million-year entries borrowed the present preset and therefore reloaded today's photographic map and city lights. Added a separate temperate procedural mode and a regression test reserving the photographic mode for the actual present.
- Corrected the red-giant disc chronology: early future eras were ramping `visual.sun` from ~+500 Myr, so the swollen Sun appeared billions of years before it leaves the main sequence. Kept planet heating via `heat`, held the disc near zero until `core-hydrogen-ends`, and stopped treating the sterile dry-cinder / late main-sequence stops as red-giant mode.
- Decoupled the Moon’s orange glow from Earth’s climate `heat`. Future Moon heating now tracks the expanding Sun (scaled onto the CSS glow threshold), so the Moon stays grey through ocean loss and only reddens once solar expansion begins; early impact/molten eras still use heat.
- Strengthened future Moon glow mapping so +5.4 BY (core hydrogen ends) is a clear visible start (~0.75 heat / ~0.4 overlay opacity), not a barely-crossed threshold.
- Anchored the reader's place in the story across viewport changes. Progress was derived from the pixel offset into the timeline, and both the viewport height and the timeline's own height change on resize, so the same scroll position mapped to a different era: resizing at "the present moment" (35/62) dropped the reader at 47/62, about 1.6 billion years into the future. `update()` now records the narrative fraction (and, separately, the final-era dwell fraction, which is not narrative time), and a resize handler converts that fraction back into a scroll position before the repaint. Anchoring is skipped when the reader is above or below the timeline so a resize in the hero or conclusion does not drag them into it.
- Deferred the four photographic present-day maps (1.35 MB of the 1.9 MB build) until the present is within 0.08 of the scroll journey. They were fetched at load, so the whole deep past paid for a texture set only one era ever shows; the initial request graph is now HTML, CSS and JS only. The policy lives in `timeline.ts` as a pure `shouldLoadPresentTextures` so it is unit-tested, while `globe.ts` holds the mechanism. Until the maps decode, the present falls back to the same procedural renderer every other era uses — verified on throttled Slow 3G, where the planet reads as a plausible blue marble and then swaps to the photograph, rather than rendering as an unlit black sphere against a blank sampler.
- Gave the timeline a real accessible layer. `[data-era-copy]` carried `aria-atomic` with no live region, so nothing was ever announced; the 62-era chronological list was also `display: none` once JavaScript enhanced the page, which removed the entire content from the accessibility tree. Added a polite, atomic status region that updates only when the active era changes (not per scrolled frame), and made the enhanced fallback visually hidden instead of removed, so the full chronology stays readable by assistive technology. Confirmed in Chrome that the announcer, the `aria-hidden="false"` panel and the globe description all name the same era.
- Recorded a testing gotcha: switching Chrome DevTools emulation between mobile and desktop recreates the document, which resets page state and re-runs `initTimeline`. That looked like a texture regression on resize until a sentinel on `window` showed the document was gone. Genuine resize behaviour has to be checked with a same-class viewport change (e.g. 1920x1080 to 1200x800), where the document survives.
- Made the timeline meter navigable instead of decorative. The rail already rendered a tick at every era's mapped position but was `aria-hidden` ornament, so the only way through 39 viewport-heights was scrolling and the whole page offered two tab stops. The twelve core eras are now real `<button>`s (`MILESTONE_ERA_IDS`, exported from `timeline.ts`), each named for its destination and revealing its short date on hover and focus; the other fifty stay decorative ticks, because sixty-two controls would sit about eight pixels apart on a rail a few hundred pixels tall and put sixty-two tab stops between the wordmark and the conclusion. Measured at 1920x1080: twelve controls, 15x14px hit areas, 23px minimum gap, so no overlap.
- Jumping had to invert the present pause. Era scroll values are story fractions, but raw scroll is stretched by the hold at the present, so a naive `era.scroll * narrativeDistance` lands short of the present and short of everything after it. Added `rawProgressForStory` as the inverse of `progressWithPresentPause`; verified in Chrome that the present, red giant, white dwarf and dinosaur controls each land on their own era, and that the present control lands inside the hold (`is-present-pause`). Jumps are instant rather than smooth — these distances run to tens of thousands of pixels and an animated scroll across that reads as a hang.
- Raised the smallest type tier. Kickers, captions, the meter and the reflection's hold indicator ran 6.7-9.3px; the floor is now about 8-10px (ten declarations, 0.42-0.58rem up to 0.5-0.62rem). Contrast already passed, so this was legibility of size only. The mobile present composition is the tight constraint: the reflection panel now clears the 390x844 viewport by 8px rather than 12px, still unclipped.
- The meter's hover/focus label was being painted over by the Moon. `.earth-figure` is z-index 2 and `.moon` is 4, while `.timeline-meter` was static/auto, so the Moon covered the left ~34px of a label that measurement said was fully on screen. The rail is an overlay control, so it now sits at z-index 5. Worth remembering that "the element's rect is inside the viewport" does not mean it is visible.
- Noted but not fixed: at 390x667 the present reflection panel overflows the viewport by about 77px. Confirmed pre-existing by overriding the type sizes back to main's values in the live page and re-measuring — main clips by 74px there, so the type bump accounts for 3px of it. 390x667 is not a marked viewport, so this is recorded rather than chased.
